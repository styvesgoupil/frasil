Playing GLINT

Turn SELECT, the KEYBOARD, to GLINT: the keys play GLINT (the badge at the top right turns blue, and says what they do: GLINT, ARP, SEQ, or REC in orange). Back to DRONE for the drones, on to BASS for the bass. A key plays an icy FM bell in the scale, OCTAVE octaves up (two by default, from +0 to +3). Pressing it makes it louder (up to +8 dB) and makes it ring longer.

VIBRATO (GLINT III, K3): once your finger has pressed in, wiggling it bends the glint’s pitch, both ways around its note, like a string player’s finger. A steady press stays in tune. Up to 25 cents at 100; about 10 at its default; OFF at 0.

Its pages

Tap ARP, then ARP again for the next page: GLINT PARAMS, GLINT II, GLINT III and GLINT DELAY, always the fourth. TYPE, on the first, says what plays GLINT besides your hands: OFF, ARP (the arpeggiator) or SEQ (the sequencer). Its pages come after GLINT DELAY: GLINT ARP for the arpeggiator, the three GLINT SEQ pages for the sequencer, none with TYPE OFF (four pages).

If the page you are on goes away (TYPE changed, a scene loaded), you land on GLINT PARAMS.

Presets

ICE, BELL, BLIP, GLASS, CHIME, DROP, FROST, CELESTA, SINE (the default), KALIMBA, MARIMBA, MUSIC BOX, VIBES, HARP, PIPE, BOWED, and six leads: WARM LEAD, BRASS, SQUARE, SUB LEAD, STRINGS, REED. A preset sets the page’s sound. Turn RATIO, BRIGHT and the rest from there.

SUSTAIN: a bell becomes a lead

SUSTAIN (GLINT III, K4) turns a bell into a lead. At 0 a glint fades out over DECAY, even while you hold its key. Turned up, it settles at that level for as long as the key is down, then rings out when you let go. DECAY is then how long it takes to settle, and PING the brightness at the start, as a filter’s sweep would be. The leads use it, with RATIO at ×1 (all the harmonics, as a saw has), ×2 (the odd ones, hollow, as a square) or ×0.5 (an octave under).

The arpeggiator

With TYPE on ARP, it plays the held GLINT keys one at a time, at RATE, over RANGE octaves, in MODE’s order. CHANCE decides whether each step plays. It follows MIDI clock when one comes in, and TEMPO (GLOBAL, K1) otherwise. It stays on the grid: a new chord, an added or a lifted note takes effect on the next RATE step, and the pattern starts again from its first note. MIDI Start resets the grid.

Latch: hold ARP. With LATCH on (the default), the arpeggio keeps playing after you lift your hands, even back on the drones. A new hand replaces the chord. Hold ARP again to turn it off. Tap PLAY and the arpeggio waits, its notes kept; tap it again and it goes on from the top. Hold PLAY to let it go with everything else.

The key whose note is sounding lights up for as long as the note lasts.

The sequencer

With TYPE on SEQ, a 32-step sequence plays GLINT in place of the arpeggio, on the same grid: RATE, MIDI clock or TEMPO, and CHANCE for each step. RANGE has no say in it. It plays on without end while TYPE is SEQ. PLAY stops it, and PLAY again starts it from its first step; MIDI Start starts it from the top too.

REC goes straight there, recording LIVE: TYPE on SEQ, the keys on GLINT, RECORD on LIVE, GLINT SEQ on screen, and the sequence started if it was stopped. REC again turns RECORD off and gives the keys back to what they played before REC’s take (RECORD OFF · DRONE); the page stays. While the sequencer records, REC flashes on the beat (twice as quick as OCT) and a metronome ticks each beat, the bar’s first higher. Hold REC to turn the metronome off, or on again; it is kept with the settings. REC keeps the sequence as it was before the take, for UNDO.

On GLINT SEQ III, NEW TAKE and UNDO turn twice the same way. NEW TAKE: every step a rest, RECORD on LIVE, the sequence started if it was stopped: play it in again from nothing. UNDO: the sequence as it was before the last take (REC, NEW TAKE) or CLEAR; again, back to the one after.

The screen draws the sequence as a constellation across the strip, 16 steps wide while LENGTH is 16 or less, else 32: a star a step, as high as its note, larger with an accent; a line for a tie, a dot for a rest; past LENGTH, grey. The step playing is white, with a line of light through it; the step on STEP has an amber mark under it, and a dotted amber line beside it with RECORD on STEP. With RECORD on LIVE, an amber light shines in the corner. The keys light the note playing, the scale’s notes faintly.

Each scene keeps its own sequence, and the settings keep the last one saved.

The delay

The delay is GLINT’s alone, on GLINT DELAY, its fourth page. TIME follows MIDI clock, or TEMPO: from 1/32 to 1/2 with triplets and dotted times, mono, then the same twelve again in ping-pong, marked PP (1/8D PP by default). Its echoes go into the cloud too; SPACE, beside them on K4, is how much of GLINT goes there.
